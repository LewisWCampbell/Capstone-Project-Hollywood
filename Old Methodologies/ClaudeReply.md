Big Tech & AI Lobbying the Trump Administration
How Much They Spent
The largest tech and AI firms collectively spent $109 million on lobbying in 2025, surpassing $100 million for the first time. Yahoo Finance
Key individual spenders:

Meta led all tech firms with a record $26.29 million in lobbying expenditures — the most it has ever spent. Issue One
Alphabet/Google spent $16.62 million, up 12% from the year before. Microsoft spent $10.1 million. Nvidia increased its lobbying spend eightfold to nearly $5 million. OpenAI spent just under $3 million, up about 70% from 2024. Raw Story
Anthropic's lobbying spending surged 511% since Trump's second term began, rising from $180,000 per quarter to $1.1 million per quarter. Washington Examiner

Beyond registered lobbying, Public Citizen calculated that Big Tech collectively spent more than $1.1 billion in total political influence spending during and since the 2024 elections — including campaign contributions, super PACs, donations to Trump's inauguration, and more. Nearly half of that (46%) came from Elon Musk alone, and nearly three-quarters (74%) favored Republicans. Public Citizen

What They Got in Return
Tax breaks are the most quantifiable payoff. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Tesla — whose CEOs all attended Trump's 2025 inauguration — collectively reported $315 billion in U.S. profits in 2025 but paid just 4.9% in federal corporate income taxes, amounting to a collective $51 billion in tax savings compared to the statutory 21% rate. Tesla paid exactly zero in federal corporate income taxes. ITEP
Meta specifically reported that accelerated depreciation provisions slashed its federal tax bill by $5 billion. Amazon's effective tax bill dropped from $9 billion in 2024 to $1.2 billion in 2025 — an effective rate of just 1.4%. Quartz
These savings came largely from Trump's "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, which provided:

Permanent, full and immediate deductibility of domestic R&D costs (instead of spreading them over 5–15 years), benefiting software, AI, cloud, and cybersecurity companies. RSM US
Permanent 100% bonus depreciation for qualifying property acquired after January 19, 2025 — critical for data center and AI infrastructure buildouts. BPM

Regulatory wins were also substantial:

Trump signed an executive order in December 2025 imposing a moratorium on AI regulation, blocking state-level efforts to protect consumers, children, and national security — despite roughly 80% of Americans supporting AI safety rules. Issue One
Nvidia secured the ability to resume exports of its H200 AI chips to China — a massive market opportunity — after its CEO held repeated meetings with Trump and White House officials. Yahoo Finance
Apple secured tariff relief after committing $600 billion to domestic manufacturing. Yahoo Finance

Government contracts and investment rounded out the benefits. The Pentagon awarded Anthropic a transaction agreement with a $200 million ceiling. Washington Examiner The OBBBA also opened up over $1 billion in new federal AI contracts and infrastructure investments that companies can compete for.

The Big Picture
Critics describe the pattern as transactional "pay-to-play" politics. Experts note that the likelihood of lobbying success under this administration has gone up, and the potential gains have increased, making each lobbying dollar more valuable than before — creating a self-reinforcing incentive to spend more. Raw Story Whether you view this as sensible industrial policy or a troubling fusion of corporate and government power largely depends on your political perspective, but the numbers on both sides of the ledger are striking.
